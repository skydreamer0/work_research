# Esvatin External Review Candidate Speaker Script

## Slide 1
This is the conservative version of the deck. One supportable product fact is that Esvatin is an estriol 0.5 milligram vaginal tablet. Beyond that, the scientific discussion remains mostly class-level estriol evidence. That is why the wording here is tighter and more restrained.

## Slide 2
This slide provides a simple disease foundation. GSM is associated with estrogen decline after menopause and can involve multiple genitourinary tissues. That is enough to frame the condition before moving into treatment logic.

## Slide 3
The purpose here is recognition. The symptoms listed are practical and relevant, and the important point is that patients may not always bring these issues up on their own. That makes recognition and appropriate management more important.

## Slide 4
In the conservative version, the tissue-level message should stay focused on the most supportable points. We can talk about local estrogenic tissue effects, epithelial maturation, and restoration of atrophic mucosal findings. We should avoid more detailed histology language unless it is separately verified.

## Slide 5
This is one of the strongest evidence slides because the numbers are directly supported. These are pooled findings from intravaginal estrogen studies, so they should be framed as pooled evidence and not translated into a precise product-specific performance claim.

## Slide 6
This slide supports a clear clinical link between tissue-related change and symptom improvement. The evidence supports change in vaginal pH and dyspareunia. The safest approach is to keep the wording close to what was reported and avoid unnecessary extrapolation.

## Slide 7
The key point on safety is that the reviewed evidence did not identify clinically relevant long-term serum hormone changes with vaginal estriol. That is a useful and supportable message. Stronger wording should be avoided unless separately verified.

## Slide 8
This slide keeps differentiation conservative. Mechanistic literature supports describing estriol as weak or short-acting, and vaginal-use studies support a limited systemic-hormone effect profile. That gives useful context without forcing a broad comparison claim.

## Slide 9
This slide now supports a cautious practical-use message. The evidence does not prove clinical superiority by dosage form, but it does support discussion of convenience, preference, and persistence across formats. That is the safe level for this section.

## Slide 10
The closing message keeps only the points that remain supportable after review. GSM is clinically relevant, local estrogen evidence supports measurable and symptomatic improvement, and product-specific claims should remain limited to what can actually be verified for Esvatin.
